**1. The Philosophy Behind It**

• Inspired by the **Socratic Method**: asking layered, reflective questions to clarify concepts.

• The AI is not a source of truth — it’s a mirror for your evolving thought.

• The goal is not the “answer” but the _clarity of thinking_.

---

**2. The Five Phases of Socratic Prompting**

  

**🧩 Phase 1: The Raw Inquiry**

  

Start with the open, messy, ambiguous question. You’re not trying to be efficient — you’re trying to _think aloud_.

  

Example prompt:

  

> “I’m trying to understand the real reason documentation gets ignored in most tech teams. What might I be missing?”

  

**🔍 Phase 2: Reflective Clarification**

  

Now treat the AI response like a philosophical opponent. Don’t accept it — _interrogate_ it.

  

Prompts:

  

> “What assumptions are built into that answer?”

> “How would this change if I were in a startup vs enterprise?”

> “Can you reframe that through a psychological rather than organizational lens?”

  

**🪞 Phase 3: Personal Synthesis**

  

Bring it back to your own experience. This is the _integration phase_.

  

Prompts:

  

> “That reminds me of when I rewrote a wiki page last month. Can you help me extract a pattern from that?”

> “If I were to turn this into a rule I follow in my work, how would it sound?”

  

**🛠️ Phase 4: Operationalization**

  

Convert insight into action. Use the AI to translate thought into structured output.

  

Prompts:

  

> “Based on this, write me a checklist for future docs I write.”

> “What would a framework for this process look like?”

  

**🔄 Phase 5: Recursive Loop**

  

Start over — now from a more evolved perspective.

  

Prompt:

  

> “What question _should_ I be asking next, now that I understand this?”

---

**3. Principles for Effective Socratic Prompting**

|**Principle**|**Explanation**|
|---|---|
|**Delay the answer**|Ask more questions _before_ trying to solve or write anything.|
|**Challenge assumptions**|Ask: “What’s implicit here?” or “Who might disagree with this and why?”|
|**Zoom in/out**|Switch perspectives: technical, philosophical, practical, psychological.|
|**Anchor in experience**|Relate concepts to your lived work or emotions. That’s where meaning lies.|
|**Translate thought**|Turn reflection into action: checklists, frameworks, guiding questions.|
|**Be recursive**|Every insight is a seed for the next question. Never stop looping.|

### 🧠 My Socratic Workflow in Practice

1. **Read the existing doc/code/requirement**
2. **Prompt: "What’s unclear?" → GPT lists issues**
3. **Prompt: "How would you rewrite it for clarity?"**
4. **Prompt: "What would you add to make it actionable?"**
5. **Use answers to rewrite, refactor, or restructure**
6. **Repeat this loop 3–5x until clarity emerges**

Every round teaches me more about the system, deepens the output, and allows me to write something that feels like it *thinks.*
