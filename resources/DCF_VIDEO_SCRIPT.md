## Full Script

### INTRODUCTION (2 minutes)

**[HOOK]**

Here's something most people get wrong about AI.

They think the goal is to write better prompts. To get better outputs. To make AI do more for them.

But that's like saying the goal of having a conversation is to get the other person to talk more.

The real opportunity isn't getting better at *using* AI. It's getting better at *thinking with* AI.

That's what the Dialectical Cognition Framework—DCF—is about.

**[INTRO SELF/CHANNEL - Optional]**

[Your standard intro]

**[PREVIEW]**

In this video, I'm going to show you:
1. Why most AI collaboration falls short
2. The single most important mental shift you need to make
3. A practical framework you can start using today

Let's dive in.

---

### THE PROBLEM (2 minutes)

**[COMMON PATTERN]**

Here's how most people use AI:

They have a question. They ask AI. They get an answer. They move on.

Or: They have a task. They prompt AI. They get output. They accept it—or they're frustrated and give up.

What's wrong with this?

**[THE ISSUE]**

Two things.

First, you're treating AI like a vending machine. Put in a request, get out a product. No relationship, no development, no growth.

Second, and this is the big one: *you're not getting any smarter*.

You got an answer, but you didn't gain any understanding. You got output, but you didn't build any capability.

Next time you face a similar problem, you're back at square one.

**[THE MISSED OPPORTUNITY]**

The real opportunity with AI isn't outsourcing your thinking.

It's *augmenting* your thinking.

Using AI as a thinking partner, not a task executor.

That's where DCF comes in.

---

### THE CORE CONCEPT (3 minutes)

**[THE MENTAL MODEL]**

DCF is built on one central metaphor: AI as a *thinking mirror*.

When you look in a physical mirror, you see yourself reflected back. But you see yourself differently than you do in your mind.

A thinking mirror does the same thing for your ideas.

When you articulate your thoughts to AI, and AI responds, you see your thinking reflected back. Transformed. Clarified. Sometimes challenged.

**[THE SHIFT]**

This requires a shift in how you approach AI.

Instead of asking "What's the answer?", you ask "What am I not seeing about this problem?"

Instead of "Generate this for me", you say "Help me think through this."

Instead of accepting the first output, you engage in dialogue.

**[THE CORE LOOP]**

The basic DCF cycle has four steps:

1. **Articulate**: Express your current thinking clearly
2. **Generate**: Get AI's response
3. **Evaluate**: Critically assess what came back
4. **Refine**: Improve and iterate

This isn't complicated. But doing it *well* is a skill.

---

### THE SOCRATIC ELEMENT (2 minutes)

**[SOCRATES INTRO]**

The "dialectical" in DCF comes from Socrates.

Socrates didn't lecture. He questioned. He helped people discover what they already knew but hadn't articulated.

DCF brings that method to AI collaboration.

**[FOUR SOCRATIC MOVES]**

There are four key Socratic moves you can use:

**Elenchus** - Cross-examination.
"What assumptions are built into this approach?"
"Where could this reasoning be wrong?"

**Maieutics** - Drawing out knowledge.
"Help me express what I'm trying to say here."
"What am I really asking underneath this question?"

**Dialectic** - Examining opposing views.
"What's the strongest argument against this?"
"Who would disagree, and why?"

**Aporia** - Productive confusion.
"What am I not seeing?"
"Where am I stuck, and why?"

These aren't just prompts. They're thinking tools.

---

### PRACTICAL APPLICATION (3 minutes)

**[DEMO SETUP]**

Let me show you what this looks like in practice.

Say I'm planning a feature for my app. The standard approach would be: "AI, design a user dashboard with these requirements."

**[THE DCF WAY]**

Here's the DCF approach:

I start by articulating: "I'm trying to add a dashboard. My goal is to help users understand their usage patterns. I think they need graphs and summaries, but I'm not sure what data actually matters to them."

Notice I'm sharing my thinking, including my uncertainty.

AI responds with ideas.

Now instead of accepting, I evaluate: "What assumptions are in this design? What if users don't actually want to see their usage? What's the simplest version that tests whether this is valuable at all?"

I'm using the AI as a sparring partner for my thinking.

Through this dialogue, I often discover: my real question wasn't about dashboard design. It was about understanding what value users get from the app.

**[THE RESULT]**

What did I gain?

Not just a dashboard design—I got clarity on my actual problem. I developed my thinking. I'm better prepared for the next decision.

That's the DCF difference.

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (2 minutes)

**[ANTI-PATTERNS]**

Before I wrap up, let me flag some common mistakes:

**Socratic Theater**: Going through the motions without genuine inquiry. Asking "What assumptions?" but not actually caring about the answer.

**Mirror Narcissism**: Using AI only to confirm what you already believe. You're not growing, you're just getting validation.

**Lazy Prompting**: Vague inputs, then frustration with vague outputs. The mirror only works if you bring something to it.

**Infinite Refinement**: Endless iteration without ever shipping. Perfect is the enemy of done.

**[THE BALANCE]**

DCF isn't about making everything a philosophical dialogue.

For simple tasks, just get the output. Save the deep engagement for when it matters: complex problems, important decisions, situations where understanding is as important as output.

---

### GETTING STARTED (1 minute)

**[CALL TO ACTION]**

Here's how to start practicing DCF today:

**Start with one question.** Before you accept any AI output, ask: "What assumptions are built into this?"

Just that one question, applied consistently, will transform your AI collaboration.

The age of AI isn't about humans doing less thinking.

It's about humans thinking *better*—with new partners, new tools, new mirrors.

DCF is how you do that.
